The path from a form field to a result

3

You add a chiller. What actually changes?

Adding a machine is not a row in a list — it rewrites the plant topology. Ten objects appear, and three existing ones are edited. Nothing is simulated until you press Run; until then the diagram and the readiness checks are just reflecting the config.

Why oversizing can make things worse

Adding capacity you do not need pushes every machine down its part-load curve. A plant with three 2,100 kW chillers on a 3,430 kW peak spends the year at low PLR and returns a worse system COP than a right-sized plant. The next section shows exactly why.

4

The three curves — full load, part load, and everything between

A chiller in EnergyPlus is four numbers and three curves. The curves are dimensionless multipliers, each normalised to 1.0 at the machine's own rating point. Everything else is interpolation away from that point.

Curve 1 · capacity

CAPFT

How much cooling the machine can make right now, given the chilled-water temperature it must produce and the condenser temperature it must reject to. Hot day → less capacity.

Curve 2 · efficiency vs temperature

EIRFT

How much more power it burns per unit of cooling at those same two temperatures. This is the “lift” penalty.

Curve 3 · efficiency vs load

EIRFPLR

How much power it burns at partial load. This is the curve that decides whether four small chillers beat two big ones.

Q_available = Q_reference × CAPFT(T_chw_out , T_cond_in) PLR = Q_demand / Q_available FRAC = min(1 , PLR / PLR_min) ← below PLR_min the machine cycles Power = (Q_available / COP_reference) × EIRFT(T_chw_out , T_cond_in) × EIRFPLR(PLR) × FRAC Q_condenser = Power × f_rejected + Q_evaporator

Verbatim from ChillerElectricEIR.cc — lines 2002 and 2292. Not a paraphrase.

Why part load dominates the annual answer

A plant sits at peak load for a handful of hours a year and at 30–60% for most of it. The EIRFPLR curve is therefore worth far more to the annual total than the nameplate COP. On this machine, dropping to 50% load costs only about 38% of full-load power — a variable-speed trait. A fixed-speed machine would spend closer to 55%.

Below the minimum, machines cycle

Under PLR_min the chiller cannot modulate any further, so it switches on and off within the timestep. EnergyPlus models that with FRAC, which linearly scales power by the fraction of the step the machine is running. Efficiency stops improving and starts degrading — visible as the flat tail on the third plot.

5

Your numbers × a real machine's curves

You know your plant's capacity and COP. You almost never know its six CAPFT coefficients. So the tool mines the reference datasets EnergyPlus ships — 271 real machines from Carrier, McQuay, Multistack, Trane and York — and borrows the curve shape from the closest one.

What this means for trust

Capacity and COP are exactly what you entered. The shape of the part-load and temperature response is a real machine's, not an invention — but it is not your machine's. Pick the actual model from the catalog picker when you know it. Every substitution the builder makes is printed in Model notes on the results screen.

6

How one hour becomes a year

The curves above are evaluated at every timestep, for every machine, with temperatures that the rest of the loop is simultaneously solving. This is the loop EnergyPlus walks 35,040 times.

The honest limits

Curve shapes are a reference machine's unless you pick your own. Heat pumps use representative curves, not a catalog model. Sample load profiles are synthetic — generated from an occupancy schedule and a seasonal curve — so upload your own CSV for anything real. And a plant that cannot meet its heating load will not simulate at all: the loop temperature diverges, which is why that case is blocked before the run rather than after.

Equations verified against ChillerElectricEIR.cc (EnergyPlus 26.1.0). Curve coefficients read from DataSets/Chillers.idf as shipped with the engine. Plots below the sliders are live evaluations of those coefficients, not illustrations.
